# Wendy kitchen check-in

**Purpose:** a short alignment conversation before working assumptions quietly become permanent design decisions.  
**Updated:** September 19, 2026

This is not a list of decisions to approve. For each item, the useful answer is simply **yes / no / not sure / I care about something different**.

The text under **Current assumption** describes where the design is presently headed. If that does not sound right, this is exactly the time to say so.

## 1. What is the kitchen primarily trying to be?

**Current assumption:** This is a serious working kitchen first, while still being a warm place for people to gather. Daily cooking and cleanup should beat occasional entertaining when those goals conflict. We are *not* designing a show kitchen around maximum island size or maximum guest seating.

**Question:** Does that sound like the right hierarchy to you? When you imagine the finished kitchen, what would make you say, “Yes, this was worth doing”?

## 2. How important is the breakfast table and backyard relationship?

**Current assumption:** The kitchen table by the windows is permanent everyday infrastructure. We eat there constantly, so we are not reclaiming that area for cabinetry or a larger kitchen. In fact, part of the remodel should make the backyard connection *better*—potentially improving the windows / openings and dealing with the south-light glare that currently makes us lower the shades.

**Question:** Is preserving and improving that everyday table/view experience as important to you as it currently is in the plan? Would you spend meaningful remodel dollars on a better backyard connection even if it buys no additional kitchen storage?

## 4. Is it okay for the chef island to be unapologetically a work surface?

**Current assumption:** The main island is relatively compact and optimized around cooking: prep sink, cutting, garbage / compost, mise en place and movement to the cooktop. It is not primarily guest seating or a decorative centerpiece.

A dark soapstone top is a serious possibility. It is extremely forgiving of heat, acid, wine and food, but it will acquire fine scratches and small marks over time.

**Question:** Do you like the idea of the central island looking increasingly like a beautiful *used* workbench, or would visible scratches bother you enough that we should choose a harder, more pristine-looking surface?

## 5. What is the guest perch actually for?

**Current assumption:** The perch is where a couple of people sit with wine and appetizers while cooking is happening. Two seats should feel natural every day; two additional stools can appear when we need four. It is not meant to replace the kitchen table or become another full dining table.

**Question:** Is that how you picture using it? Are there situations you expect—breakfast, laptop use, full dinners, grandchildren someday, something else—that would change what this surface needs to do?

## 6. Should the guest perch feel like furniture rather than kitchen cabinetry?

**Current assumption:** We are deliberately trying to avoid “two islands.” The perch would be a lighter, more furniture-like object—potentially with a beautiful wood top rather than stone, no waterfall edge, and a base designed more like furniture than another cabinet block.

A wood top would be finished so guests can put down wineglasses and food without placemats, though it would still age more like good furniture than stone.

**Question:** Does that distinction appeal to you? When you picture the room, would you rather the perch visually disappear into the cabinetry, or feel like a separate furniture piece?

**Wendy:** ☐ furniture-like ☐ more integrated with kitchen ☐ unsure

## 7. How much hosting choreography feels reasonable?

**Current assumption:** We host often enough to design for it, but not enough to let peak-party conditions dominate every day. The current thinking accepts things like bringing out two extra stools for guests, possibly moving one kitchen-table chair in one layout, and having the cook briefly yield an aisle during serving. What we *do* want is separate buffet/staging space so guests do not have to vacate the perch when dinner is served.

**Question:** Does occasional setup / chair movement feel perfectly reasonable to you, or do you want the kitchen to support four perch guests and serving with essentially zero rearranging?

**Wendy:** ☐ some choreography is fine ☐ minimize it aggressively ☐ unsure

## 8. Which spatial feeling matters more: openness or more continuous counter?

**Current assumption:** The current practical lead is a freestanding 72 × 42 perch (F2) because it preserves a more straightforward open route through the kitchen. A serious alternative (P2) connects the social/helper surface toward the stairs and gains substantially more useful counter beside the cooktop, but it makes circulation more dependent on opening the old porch and on hosting setup.

**Question:** At a gut level, which sounds more valuable to you: **a more open, freestanding room** or **a more connected, enveloping work area with additional counter**? No need to choose a floor plan yet; this is about the feeling you prefer.

**Wendy:** ☐ openness / freestanding ☐ more continuous workspace ☐ need to see both mocked up

## 9. Does the emerging visual/material direction feel like us?

**Current assumption:** Warm wood cabinetry with quiet grain, inset / beaded detailing, restrained hardware, fewer upper cabinets in the main kitchen, and more contrast than the typical cream-on-cream luxury kitchen.

The current countertop hypothesis is:
- quieter, lighter natural stone / quartzite around the perimeter;
- dark soapstone on the chef island;
- furniture-grade wood on the guest perch.

The intention is richness from real materials, not from lots of decorative detail.

**Question:** Does that sound attractive to you? What part makes you excited, and what part makes you worry the kitchen could become too dark, too woody, too traditional, too busy, or simply not your taste?

**Wendy:** ☐ broadly yes ☐ broadly no ☐ need to see a palette together

## 10. Where do you want convenience, even if Jon does not particularly care about it?

**Current assumption:** We are being skeptical about appliances and features that sound luxurious but do not earn their space. Induction is the leading cooktop direction; steam-combi is under serious consideration; warming drawer and blast chiller remain unproven; instant boiling water is not currently considered necessary. The beverage room is intended partly to move everyday water / coffee / ice traffic away from the cooking zone.

## Design implications

The strongest benefit is a **side destination for repeated trips**: breakfast equipment/supplies, water, cold beverages, mugs/glasses and associated cleanup can be considered together so users need fewer return trips to the main kitchen. Storage for these functions is a design suggestion; the specific appliance list is still exploratory.

The table-facing opening could let table-side users reach that service zone, while the stair-base opening could support another approach. These are proposed circulation opportunities, not yet proven clear paths. Do not assume all existing trips disappear or require guest seating to move into the reclaimed room.

Reduced beverage traffic gives a continuous range-side peninsula a fairer operational test. It does **not** resolve its geometry automatically: the remaining stair arrival, table-to-range-aisle-to-Butler's-pantry serving route, dish loading and four occupied guest places still matter.

The sketch creates no new reason to reduce the agreed island or daily cleanup allowance. It also does not prove a continuous peninsula fits. The old connected rectangle's conflict and the later freestanding tests must remain specific findings, not universal conclusions.

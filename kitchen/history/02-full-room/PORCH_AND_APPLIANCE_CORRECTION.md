Jon supplied these corrections after the first full-room drawing. They supersede the affected drawing assumptions.

## Two separate screened porches

The new, large screened porch lies beyond M5, the French doors and the large sliding glass doors. Jon and Wendy love it. It is retained and is not the area to reclaim.

The old, small screened porch is accessed by a door at the bottom of the kitchen stairs. Its reported dimensions are **9 ft 3 in × 13 ft 5 in = 111 × 161 inches**. Their orientation and inside/outside endpoints have not yet been registered to the kitchen coordinate frame.

Jon hopes to remove the wall with windows facing the kitchen table, plus as much of the stair-base wall/door as feasible. This is a desired opening strategy, not a finding of structural feasibility.

Its purpose is a **breakfast and beverage service area, not an eating area**. Possible equipment includes breakfast supplies/appliances, a beverage refrigerator, perhaps a dishwasher, an ice machine and a water dispenser. The equipment list is exploratory. Guests can get beverages without entering the cooking zone; Wendy can get water and other everyday items without repeated main-fridge trips. Reducing daily kitchen traffic is a central benefit.

The room is off to the side. Do not assume the guest perch or guest overflow extends into it. Withdraw the previous recommendation to test guest expansion into a porch transition at M5: that recommendation conflated the two porches.

## Cooking equipment and ventilation

There is **no oven below the existing cooking unit**. It has downdraft ventilation. The existing ovens are on the appliance wall, which remains part of the whole-room design even though recent studies focused on the center.

Whether a future hood is feasible, or downdraft must remain, is unresolved. Do not assume either a hood or a future oven under the cooking unit.

Withdraw the drawing’s range-oven door/operator obstruction as a statement about current conditions. The dishwasher and guest/table/stair diagnostics are separate and remain qualified by their stated assumptions. Appliance-wall oven and refrigerator operating envelopes still need to be tested in a concrete proposal.

## Continuous peninsula

Jon has not abandoned the continuous-peninsula concept. The recent freestanding studies were alternatives, not a decision. An earlier particular range-connected rectangle conflicted with the stair reference; that does not prove every continuous shape impossible.

The next comparison should distinguish the value of continuous range-side mise/landing and a helper/guest connection from its impact on the remaining table/stair-to-kitchen-and-pantry route. Beverage traffic can be redirected by the reclaimed room, but that does not erase the physical route used for arrival, serving and dining.

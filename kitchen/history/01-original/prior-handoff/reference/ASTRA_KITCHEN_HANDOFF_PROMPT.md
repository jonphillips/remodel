# Prompt for a fresh kitchen-design collaborator

I am asking you to join an ongoing kitchen redesign as an independent, critical design partner. Please read the project files before offering a solution, especially:

1. `KITCHEN_CANONICAL_FILES.md`
2. `kitchen_design_brief_existing_conditions_v1_1.md`
3. `kitchen_existing_conditions_geometry_v1_1.json`
4. `kitchen_scaled_base_plan_survey_v1.md`
5. `kitchen_design_history_options_v1.md`
6. `kitchen_flipped_peninsula_cleanup_diagonal_study_v1.md`
7. `kitchen_reference_images.md` and its two retained photos

Treat the documents as authoritative about what is **LOCKED TOPOLOGY**, what is a **LOCKED MEASUREMENT**, and what remains **UNSURVEYED / SCHEMATIC**. Do not use topology v4, any photo, or any concept drawing as a scaled tracing underlay. Do not quietly turn a provisional coordinate test into an as-built fact.

The core goal is a serious, highly personal one-cook kitchen with real helper surge and a distinct social guest-perch layer—without chairs at the island. Protect the dining-table/window relationship. We are empty nesters, so child traffic is not the overriding issue, but the family-room / kitchen / dining route must feel natural. Facing the range is desirable but not dogma; comfortable pivot cooking can be acceptable if the whole room works better. The existing range should be held in the current study because exhaust relocation may be difficult.

The current promising hypothesis is **not an approved plan**: reclaim today’s diagonal family-room-side passageway for a straighter cleanup/service row, shift that row outward toward the family-room wall if the field condition allows it, and use a range-anchored flipped peninsula for chef support plus a raised guest layer. The test produces nominal 44 in at the table corner and 50 in beyond the peninsula end, but those numbers require field verification.

Please do the following:

1. Start by stating your understanding of the kitchen in 6–10 compact bullets, explicitly separating facts from inferences.
2. Ask any follow-up questions needed to avoid a false precise plan. Focus questions on measurements or operational priorities that would materially change the recommendation; do not ask for information already present in the files.
3. Give a candid critique of the active reclaim-the-diagonal idea. Identify both its potential and its failure modes, especially circulation, family-room encroachment, services, and whether it truly produces better chef work surface rather than simply a smaller guest perch.
4. Present two or three coherent options, including the active idea and at least one materially different alternative. For each, explain the operational logic, what it improves, what it sacrifices, the measurements it depends on, and what a scaled test would have to prove.
5. Push back when the evidence warrants it. Do not acquiesce to earlier enthusiasm or defend a concept merely because it is already drawn.
6. Keep the eventual deliverable in view: a reliable 3D model must eventually be built from a real field survey, not from conceptual geometry.

# Reclaim-the-Diagonal Cleanup Study — v1

**Status:** Active concept test. Not an existing-condition revision, scaled construction plan, or approved kitchen layout.  
**Purpose:** Test whether the present diagonal family-room passageway can become productive cleanup/service counter without sacrificing a natural route from the dining table into the kitchen.

## The idea being tested

Keep the range where it is for this test because its exhaust path makes relocation difficult. Replace the assumption that today’s diagonal cleanup/peninsula geometry must remain with a cleaner division of labor:

1. A range-anchored, flipped peninsula supplies chef support and a distinct raised guest bar.
2. The cleanup sink/dishwasher move to a straighter family-room-side cleanup row.
3. That cleanup row **cheats toward the family-room wall into what is now the diagonal passageway**.
4. The lower end of the cleanup row stays outboard of the dining-table approach, leaving a real open route from table to inner kitchen.

This is not “make the guest perch smaller.” Its point is to turn presently awkward diagonal space into useful service counter while drawing the circulation route first.

## What this study preserves

- A serious chef-support counter connected to the held range.
- A separate guest social layer rather than chairs around the island.
- The current island as a reference point only; no claim that its current size or location is final.
- A direct table-to-inner-kitchen route; the ordinary open floor is not marked or treated as a special-use “pivot area.”
- The dining table/window relationship: no solution may push the table toward the windows.

## What is deliberately unresolved

- Whether the wall/family-room boundary has the 5 in of available room required for the cleanup-row move.
- The exact row length and its lower endpoint: extra length belongs in the former diagonal footprint, not in the dining approach.
- Table and chair envelope, turning clearances, and the true narrowest walkable passage.
- Plumbing, dishwasher, electrical, cabinet, and ventilation feasibility.
- Final island dimensions, prep-sink placement, and whether the island or chef support takes the primary prep role.

## Decision rule

Keep this concept alive only if field checks confirm a naturally usable table-to-kitchen route while the shifted cleanup row can be built without unacceptable family-room encroachment. If it fails, that is useful: it means the diagonal area cannot pay for the counter program, rather than merely proving that the guest perch needs to shrink.

## Required next field checks

1. Measure the maximum feasible cleanup-counter outer face along the family-room side, with wall/trim/opening constraints identified.
2. Locate the lower cleanup endpoint and verify the route from that point to the table’s actual corner and chair envelope.
3. Confirm the narrowest clear walking width and the turning condition; 42 in at the table corner is the minimum test threshold.
4. Confirm plumbing, drain, dishwasher, electrical, and cabinet-depth feasibility for the new cleanup run.

Until then, this document and its image are an honest spatial hypothesis—not a tracing underlay for 2D drawings or the eventual 3D model.
